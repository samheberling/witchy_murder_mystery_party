Why we're gathering

The Séance Ritual

The Ashgrove Coven gathers tonight for a multi-purpose séance:

  • The annual replenishment. Hallow's Eve is the year's largest night for restoring the coven's shared magical reserve, which is drawn down all year and refilled through communal ritual.
  • A succession. Ottilie Thorne, the coven's longtime Seat-holder, is ready to step down. Tonight's séance ritual is expected to name whoever leads the coven next.

The full communal-chalice séance opens the veil far enough to consult the coven's ancestors. It is only performed on nights when something this big is at stake. Most of the coven has never seen it done and doesn't know the procedure.

Before you arrive

  • You are a member (or guest) of the Ashgrove Coven, gathered at the coven's shared hall for the séance ritual.
  • Everyone here belongs to a House — a magical bloodline. Yours is noted on your character card.
  • You know the people in the Who's Who tab, and roughly how you relate to them.
  • The Family & Coven Web visually explains everyone's relation to each other.
  • Come ready to mingle. The night unfolds from there.
The World

Rules of the Witch World

A fully magical world, separate from any human one. No humans exist here — every character is a magical being with very human motivations.

Houses & Bloodlines +

Power belongs to Houses — bloodlines — not individuals. A House's standing is determined by the strength of its ancestral magic.

  • House Thorne — currently holds the Ashgrove Seat, under Ottilie.
  • House Vane — an old House that once had a claim on the Ashgrove Seat. The House is presumed extinguished.
  • House Faircloth — known for witnessing and truth-magic. Its heirs serve as the coven's Witness, whose word makes a succession official.
  • House Ashby — known for experimenting with dark magic.
  • House Bellweather — a modest House known for protective charms, herbal remedies, and assimilating new members.
  • House Cross — a minor House known for curiosity and high intellect.
Lifespans +
  • Witches and wizards don't age the way humans do. They often live to 600 years or more.
How the Coven Works +
  • The Seat anchors the coven. It's a physical and magical central connecting point that's held in trust with the ancestors, not owned outright.
  • Multiple Houses, one coven. Each keeps its own bloodline identity and internal hierarchy, but all contribute to and draw from the shared magic reserve during joint rituals.
  • Succession isn't strictly hereditary. When a Seat-holder steps down, the coven's spirits choose the next leader from any eligible member House. In practice, the outgoing Seat-holder presents a name and the spirits usually confirm it. A succession has three parts: the Seat-holder presents a name, the spirits confirm it, and the coven's Witness (held by House Faircloth) records it. Without the Witness's official sign-off, the succession is not confirmed.
  • The priestess keeps the coven's rites. She prepares the séance rituals and tends the altar. She is often the first to sense when the ancestors are uneasy. She serves the Seat-holder and the ancestors but does not lead the coven. Percy Thorne holds it now.
  • Coven law forbids open aggression between member Houses. Rivalry stays political like: swaying influence, performing séance rituals, alliances, and maintaining a good reputation.
The Ministry of Magic (played by Sam and E) +

The Ministry of Magic oversees every coven. It regulates how much a coven may draw from its shared reserve and requires certain records to be formally filed rather than just remembered by a family. Think of it like a city hall mixed with the police department. Its recognition is what makes a succession official beyond the coven itself. They can answer any questions you might have.

Magic: The Give and Take +
  • Every casting draws from a magic reserve. Reserves refill only through ritual: séance rituals tied to the land, the moon, or ancestral energy.
  • Hallow's Eve is the year's largest replenishment night.
  • Overspending a reserve draws backlash on the caster.
The Veil +
  • The Veil separates the living and the dead.
  • Major ritual nights, like Hallow's Eve, thin the Veil enough to feel the ancestors' spirits more strongly.
  • A séance ritual asks the ancestors for guidance. Forcing an ancestor's will with private magic is forbidden by coven law. It costs a huge amount of magic reserve and brings heavy backlash. The caster may feel tired, get cold hands, headaches and misfiring magic. Any family line the magic is aimed at can feel the same symptoms and have vivid dreams.

Quick Cheat Sheet

  • No humans exist in this world. Every character is a magical being of some House.
  • Magic always has a cost — it draws from a reserve that must be ritually replenished.
  • Power is inherited through bloodline (House), not just individual talent.
  • Multiple Houses share a single coven and its Seat. Rivalry is political, not violent. Coven law forbids using dark magic against fellow member Houses.
